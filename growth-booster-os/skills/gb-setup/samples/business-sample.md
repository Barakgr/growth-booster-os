# Suncoast Comfort Air — the business

## What we do

We fix, replace, and maintain residential AC systems in north Pinellas County, Florida. Based in Palm Harbor. Eleven years in, about $900K a year, six people. Summer is repair calls; replacements come when a system finally gives out. No commercial, no new construction.

## What we call the people we serve

"homeowner" (singular), "homeowners" (plural). Use these words, not "customers" or "clients".

## Services and rough prices

- Diagnostic visit — $89, waived if we do the repair
- AC repair — most jobs $250 to $900; capacitors on the low end, blower motors and coils on the high end
- System replacement — $6,500 to $14,000 installed, depending on size and efficiency. Never quote this without a visit.
- Maintenance plan — $189/yr, two tune-ups, priority scheduling, 10% off repairs
- Duct cleaning — $450 to $700 for a typical house

## Where our homeowners are

Palm Harbor, Dunedin, Tarpon Springs, Oldsmar, East Lake, Safety Harbor, north Clearwater. We'll go to Trinity if it's a replacement. We don't go south of Belleair or across the bridge to Tampa.

## Who the ideal homeowner is and why they call now

Owns a single-family house built 1985 to 2010, system is 10 to 15 years old, had a regular AC guy and just lost him (retired, sold, stopped answering). Usually 45 to 70. Wants someone who shows up when they say they will.

They call because the house is 84 degrees at 6 PM in July and the unit outside is humming but nothing's blowing.

## Top pains, in their words

1. "The last company told me I needed a whole new system and I didn't trust them."
2. "Nobody calls me back."
3. "The guy showed up three hours late and didn't tell me."
4. "I don't know if this price is fair."

## How leads arrive

- Google Business Profile calls — 15 to 25 a week in summer, 6 to 10 in winter. Biggest source.
- Website form — 3 to 5 a week. Karen gets an email.
- Nextdoor — 2 to 4 a week, usually a neighbor tagging us on a recommendation thread.
- Referrals from maintenance plan members — 2 to 3 a week. Best leads we get.
- Yelp — maybe 1 a month. Not worth much.

## What happens after a lead comes in

Karen answers the phone 8 to 5, Monday to Friday. She books the visit in the CRM calendar, texts the homeowner a confirmation, and the tech gets it on his phone. Website form leads go to Karen's email; she calls back within the hour if she sees it.

After 5 PM and on weekends, calls go to voicemail. Dave checks it when he gets home, sometimes not until morning. Some of those people have already called someone else.

Where it breaks down: after-hours calls. We lose maybe 5 to 8 leads a week in summer to voicemail. Nobody texts them back. Also, replacement quotes sit for weeks with no follow-up unless Dave remembers.

## How we ask for reviews

Where reviews live: Google. Current: 4.7 stars, 84 reviews. Facebook has a handful, Yelp has 6.

How we ask: when Dave remembers, in person at the end of a good job. Karen has texted the link a few times. No system.
