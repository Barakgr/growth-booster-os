# Follow-up playbook

**The leaky bucket.** Most owner-run businesses don't have a lead problem; they have a bucket with holes. Leads come in the top (calls, forms, referrals) and leak out the bottom (unanswered voicemail, quotes nobody chased, customers who forgot you). Patching holes beats pouring more in.

**Speed to lead.** A plain reply in minutes beats a perfect reply tomorrow. Someone who reaches out is ready now and probably called two other businesses too. Whoever answers first usually wins, so the first touch is always "now", and short.

Every sequence stops at three touches: heard, not pestered. Examples use the sample business (Dave, Suncoast Comfort Air).

---

## 1. Missed call

**Why it leaks:** Voicemail means they dial the next name. Most never leave a message.

**Sequence:**
1. Now — text (within 5 minutes if possible), and call back
2. Next business morning — call, then email if no answer
3. Day 3 — last text

**The message must:** apologize once, briefly, and ask what they need.

Good: `Hi Maria, it's Dave from Suncoast Comfort Air — sorry we missed you. What's going on with the AC? Call or text anytime today. — Dave`

Bad: `Dear valued customer, thank you for contacting Suncoast Comfort Air! Please call back at your earliest convenience!` (Template opener. Exclamation points. No name, no question.)

**Stop rule:** After the day-3 text, mark no response.

## 2. Form or web inquiry not answered

**Why it leaks:** A form fill is someone typing their problem at 10 PM. Noon tomorrow loses to whoever answered at 10:04.

**Sequence:**
1. Now — text; email the same minute if that's all you have
2. Tomorrow 10 AM — email
3. Day 3 — last text

**The message must:** prove a human read it. Repeat their words back in one phrase.

Good: `Hi Tom, Dave here from Suncoast. Saw your note about the upstairs not cooling. Does tomorrow morning or afternoon work better for a look? — Dave`

Bad: `We received your inquiry and a member of our team will be in touch shortly.` (Promises a reply instead of being one. No name, no question.)

**Stop rule:** After the day-3 text, mark no response.

## 3. Quote or estimate sent, gone quiet

**Why it leaks:** The owner reads silence as no. Usually it means busy, waiting on a spouse, or comparing quotes.

**Sequence:** Slower; give them room.
1. Now — text (once the quote is 2+ days old)
2. Day 3 — email
3. Day 7 — last text
Never re-quote lower in a follow-up.

**The message must:** make it easy to say "not yet" or ask a question. No pressure.

Good: `Hi Linda, Dave from Suncoast. Checking in on the estimate for the new unit — any questions? Happy to walk through it. No rush. — Dave`

Bad: `Hi Linda! Just circling back on your quote. Pricing is only valid through Friday, so let me know ASAP!` ("Circling back". Fake deadline. Pressure.)

**Stop rule:** After the day-7 text, mark no response; reactivate list in 60 days.

## 4. Appointment no-show

**Why it leaks:** Embarrassment on both sides. Nobody calls; the job disappears.

**Sequence:**
1. Within an hour of the missed window — text
2. Tomorrow morning — call, then email
3. Day 3 — last text

**The message must:** assume something came up and offer to rebook. Zero blame.

Good: `Hi Sam, Dave here — looks like we missed each other this morning. No problem. Want to grab another time this week? — Dave`

Bad: `Our technician arrived at 9 AM and no one was home. Contact our office to reschedule. Repeat no-shows may incur a fee.` (A warning letter. Blame first, threat last.)

**Stop rule:** After the day-3 text, mark no response.

## 5. Job done, no review

**Why it leaks:** "When I remember" is never. The best moment is the day the job finishes; the chance drops daily after that.

**Sequence:**
1. Same day, after the job — text with the review link
2. Day 3 — one reminder text
3. Stop. Never a third ask.

**The message must:** name the job, carry the link. One ask, no bribe.

Good: `Hi Priya, Dave here. Glad we got the compressor sorted today. If you've got a minute, a quick Google review helps a lot: [link]. Thanks either way. — Dave`

Bad: `We hope you enjoyed your experience! Please leave us a 5-star review at the link below!` (Asks for 5 stars, which review sites forbid. No name, no job detail.)

**Stop rule:** After the day-3 reminder, stop. No asks for a year.

## 6. Past customer to reactivate

**Why it leaks:** A happy customer from two years ago has forgotten your name. Not lost, just not thinking of you. One nudge brings some back for almost nothing.

**Sequence:**
1. Now — text
2. Day 5 — email
3. Stop.

**The message must:** remind them who you are and what you did, plus one true reason to call now (season, service due, an offer from business.md).

Good: `Hi Carlos, Dave from Suncoast — we replaced your AC in 2024. Summer's coming; want a tune-up before it gets ugly? — Dave`

Bad: `Hi Carlos! We miss you! Book our limited-time offer before it's gone!` ("We miss you" is fake. Urgency, no real offer.)

**Stop rule:** After the day-5 email, mark no response; one retry in 6 months.

## 7. Referral introduced

**Why it leaks:** The warmest lead there is, and the fastest to go cold, because the referrer said "he'll call you." If he doesn't, both notice.

**Sequence:**
1. Now — text, naming the referrer
2. Tomorrow — call, then email
3. Day 3 — last text
Thank the referrer same day.

**The message must:** name the referrer in the first line and ask what they need.

Good: `Hi Jen, Dave from Suncoast. Karen Mills mentioned you might need a hand with your AC. What's going on? — Dave`

Bad: `You've been referred to Suncoast Comfort Air. Please visit our website to schedule a consultation.` (No referrer named. A website instead of a person.)

**Stop rule:** After the day-3 text, mark no response; tell the referrer you tried.
